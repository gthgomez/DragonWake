Status: **FROZEN** — current product-direction law.

This is a scoped amendment of
[`DIRECTION_FREEZE_V1.md`](./DIRECTION_FREEZE_V1.md). It does not rewrite
the v1.0 freeze. Every v1.0 protection remains in force unless this file
explicitly amends it.

Authority: [`CANON_AUTHORITY.md`](./CANON_AUTHORITY.md).
Product design: [`DRAGON_DRIVEN_EMPIRE_CANON.md`](./DRAGON_DRIVEN_EMPIRE_CANON.md).
Implemented state: [`../CURRENT_STATE.md`](../CURRENT_STATE.md).

## What v1.1 does not reopen

The following remain **CANON — FROZEN** exactly as written in v1.0:

- low-to-medium fantasy; mundane world first
- late-medieval military foundation; gunpowder is not dominant
- a dragon is never merely another troop type
- no `Dragon × 28,492` army stacks
- dragon origins remain UNKNOWN; dragons predate surviving kingdoms
- no chromatic elemental-faction taxonomy
- dragon anatomy and hunting matter
- intelligence spectrum; True Dragons choose alliances
- Dragon Slayers are knowledge traditions, not +9000 heroes
- human armies remain necessary
- magic is rare
- PvP is strategic loss, not account destruction
- the first major bond is earned; not granted in the first tutorial minutes
- no dragon gacha
- no collectible rarity ladder as the product
- Power Score is not the main strategy mechanic
- evolutionary migration of the existing MMORTS loop, not a rewrite

v1.0 §27 (explicitly removed from direction) remains in force.

## Amendment 1 — Product identity

**v1.0 one-sentence direction (preserved):**

> Dragon Wake is a persistent medieval strategy world where historically grounded kingdoms have spent centuries adapting their armies, castles, cultures and beliefs to the reality of rare, terrifying and sometimes intelligent dragons.

**v1.1 product identity (added, FROZEN):**

> Dragons don't decorate your empire in DragonWake. They are one of the forces that determine what your empire becomes.

DragonWake is a persistent medieval strategy / MMORTS in which discovering,
understanding, raising, bonding with, negotiating with, hunting, equipping,
and deploying dragons determines how the player's civilization expands,
researches, fights, and survives.

This is **not**:

- a single-companion-dragon RPG
- a Dragons of Atlantis clone
- Reign of Atlantis with renamed elements
- a collectible dragon roster
- a gacha system
- a conventional research tree of mostly percentage bonuses
- a finite six-act campaign with nothing meaningful after the last authored dragon

---

## Amendment 2 — Dragons as reasons for civilization

**v1.0 §19 loop (preserved as the MMORTS spine):** build, produce, defend,
research, recruit, explore, fight PvE, take territory, join alliances, wage
wars.

**v1.1 amendment (FROZEN):**

> Discovering or establishing a relationship with dragons may unlock or transform settlements and map capabilities.

**Progression thesis (FROZEN):**

> Dragons are not rewards attached to settlements. Dragons are one of the primary reasons new settlements and new forms of civilization become possible.

The authored holding ladder is a spine, not a sticker book of cities. A
holding must feel like a civilization shaped by the dragon, terrain,
resources, and problems around it.

---

## Amendment 3 — Three-layer dragon hierarchy

**FROZEN:**

> A mature player's realm may maintain relationships with a small number of major dragons. One remains the player's signature bonded dragon, while additional domain dragons represent major empire chapters, territorial relationships, or strategic pacts. Each major dragon is a persistent strategic being with agency, identity, limitations, a meaningful relationship to place, and at most one presence per march. Major dragons are not a collectible rarity ladder. Wild dragons remain predominantly unbound fauna.

### Layer A — Signature Dragon

Exactly one primary protagonist dragon per player/account at a time unless
a later freeze explicitly changes this.

Player fantasy: *this is my dragon.*

It begins as a hatchling, is named by the player, grows visibly, acquires
history, and remains relevant after other dragons enter the empire.

### Layer B — Domain Dragons

Approximately 4–7 additional major dragons across a mature account. Exact
count is adjustable. This is not an alpha implementation requirement.

Each is individually important, rare, persistent, strategically meaningful,
and tied to a domain, settlement, geography, political relationship, or
major world capability. They are more than equipment slots or stat
modifiers.

They do **not** all hatch from eggs, start young, become conventionally
owned, share UI, share harness slots, or unlock a settlement.

**Instancing (Alpha+):** ordinary domain dragons are persistent **local
individuals** instantiated from a species/archetype. Do not give every
player the same globally unique named adult. Realm-scale Great Dragons
(Old Karth class) may later be truly shared; they are not in Alpha.

### Layer C — Wild Dragon Ecology

The world contains dragons that do not belong to the player: lesser drakes,
wyverns, regional predators, migratory dragons, named wild individuals,
colossal creatures, mysterious species, realm-level threats.

Most wild dragons never become permanent player assets.

## Amendment 4 — World verbs, not percentage identities

**FROZEN:**

Every major dragon must unlock or alter at least one **map verb**: a way of
interacting with territory that existed *before* the dragon was acquired.

Rejected as an entire identity:

- `+15% march speed`
- `+20% defense`
- any dragon whose design summary is a numeric modifier

Settlements whose entire identity is "new resource + two new troops" are
also rejected.

Research whose entire identity is "wait timer → percentage increase" is
rejected as dragon research.

---

## Amendment 5 — One major dragon per march

**FROZEN** (clarifies v1.0 §4):

One major dragon per march. A dragon is a strategic commitment.

Model at least: **home / away / wounded-or-recovering**.

These states are visible in Castle, Realm, reports, and scouting when
appropriate. Enemy knowledge that a dragon has left a holding should
matter.

---

## Amendment 6 — First dragon timing

**v1.0 §25 preserved:** the first major bond is earned. Do not grant a
dragon in the first five tutorial minutes. Early game establishes danger,
sightings, damaged settlements, incomplete knowledge, and mystery.

**v1.1 clarification:** the current Alpha expedition charter that founds
Marcher Keep is a *human frontier response*, not the signature bond.
Implementation may keep that charter. Design must not treat "expedition
complete" as "you now own a dragon."

The player should want a dragon before receiving one.

---

## Amendment 7 — Equipment changes role

Visible dragon equipment / harnesses remain part of the fantasy.

**FROZEN reject:** giant loot rarity ladders; relic duplication; random
legendary armor treadmills; shop-sold egg/armor progression; endless
`+Power` stacking.

Equipment should primarily change **role**. Species do not share identical
slot matrices.

No pay-to-skip of core injury or Titan recovery.

---

## Amendment 8 — Research emerges from observation

Dragon research is not a conventional tech tree of percentage bonuses.

Canonical loop:

> OBSERVE → HYPOTHESIZE → TEST → CODIFY

Knowledge states such as RUMORED → OBSERVED → SUPPORTED → PROVEN are the
intended model. The Bestiary is an evidence surface, not a stamp album.

Failure may produce knowledge. Farming complete knowledge from cheap
repeats is not allowed.

---

## Amendment 9 — Evergreen realm after authored progression

The authored dragon / settlement progression is the spine. It cannot be
the entire long-term game.

**FROZEN reject as default live-ops:**

- a new colored dragon every season
- gacha banners
- chest tracks as the retention engine

Named wild individuals, migrations, changing ecology, alliance conflict,
and research arms races are the intended evergreen layer.

## What v1.1 still does not freeze

Left open on purpose:

- exact domain-dragon count (4–7 is a horizon, not a quota)
- final lore names (working names in the roster are CANON — PROVISIONAL)
- exact starting-region geography (Lore Bible v1)
- numeric balance
- whether every domain dragon founds a holding (they must not)
- Titan individual name and exact provisioning numbers
- which future dragons occupy the remaining roster slots

## Product test (updated)

Keep the v1.0 test:

> Does this feature make Dragon Wake feel more like a believable medieval civilization shaped by dragons?

Add the v1.1 test:

> Does this dragon, holding, or research change what the player can *do* in the world — or is it a reskin, a percentage, or a collectible?

If the second test fails, reject the feature even if the first test is
arguable.
