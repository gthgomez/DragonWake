# Dragon Wake — Direction Freeze v1.0

Status: **FROZEN**

Dragon Wake is a persistent medieval fantasy strategy game centered on a world
shaped by the real existence of dragons.

Its primary inspiration is not modern RPG fantasy. It draws from old dragon
myths, medieval bestiaries, dragon-slayer legends, medieval warfare, castles,
feudal politics, armies, siegecraft and folklore, then builds an original
world from those foundations.

The guiding question is:

> If dragons had truly existed alongside medieval civilizations, how would
> warfare, kingdoms, religion, technology, architecture and society have
> evolved around them?

That becomes the foundation of the entire game.

This document is product + lore direction, not yet detailed canon. It exists
so future agents cannot gradually turn Dragon Wake back into generic elemental
fantasy.

---

## 1. Core fantasy — FROZEN

The player fantasy is:

> Build a medieval kingdom in a dangerous age of dragons. Raise armies,
> fortify castles, discover ancient beasts, conquer territory, fight rival
> kingdoms, hunt dragons, study them, protect them or eventually earn the
> allegiance of one.

Dragon Wake is therefore simultaneously:

Kingdom builder + MMORTS + medieval warfare game + dragon fantasy.

Dragons are not decoration. They are one of the fundamental forces shaping
civilization.

---

## 2. Tone — FROZEN

Low-to-medium fantasy.

The mundane world must remain recognizable enough for fantastical elements
to feel extraordinary.

The baseline world contains: castles; fortified towns; villages; feudal
kingdoms; knights; professional soldiers; levies; archers; crossbowmen;
pikemen; cavalry; siege warfare; trade; agriculture; mining; religion;
dynasties; mercenaries; disease; famine; diplomacy; succession; territorial
warfare.

Magic exists, but magic does not replace ordinary civilization.

A normal soldier carries steel rather than a glowing magical weapon.
A castle wall matters. Armor matters. Logistics matter. An army needs food.
A dragon appearing over that battlefield should therefore feel extraordinary.

### Explicitly rejected

Dragon Wake is not:

- Warcraft-like high fantasy
- everyone-can-use-magic fantasy
- superhero fantasy
- anime power scaling
- elemental-color faction fantasy
- a dragon collectible / gacha game
- a generic mobile city builder with dragon skins

---

## 3. Historical grounding — FROZEN

Dragon Wake uses a broad late-medieval military foundation, approximately
inspired by warfare from the 12th through 15th centuries rather than
reproducing a specific historical country or year.

This deliberately allows: longbows; crossbows; spears; pikes; swords; axes;
polearms; halberds; shields; mail; plate armor; mounted knights; siege
towers; trebuchets; ballista-like engines; fortified castles.

### Gunpowder

For the initial world:

- Gunpowder is not a dominant military technology.
- No musketeer armies.
- No cannon-dominated warfare.

This keeps dragon + bow + lance + siege engine + knight at the center of
the visual identity.

Primitive alchemical explosives may eventually exist, but they are rare and
secondary.

---

## 4. Dragons — FROZEN

This is the most important rule:

> A dragon is never merely another troop type.

Dragons are rare, extremely dangerous creatures.

An army might contain 15,000 infantry, 5,000 archers, 1,200 cavalry, 40
siege engines — and one dragon.

The dragon should materially change the battle.

There will never be `Dragon × 28,492` as a standard army stack.

---

## 5. Dragon origins — intentionally unresolved

Do not yet canonically answer where dragons ultimately came from.

Ancient civilizations disagree. Possible explanations can exist
simultaneously: creations of forgotten gods; beings older than mankind;
creatures produced by the natural world; remnants of a previous age;
descendants of primordial serpents; something stranger.

This mystery should survive for a long time.

- **FROZEN:** dragons predate all currently surviving human kingdoms.
- **NOT FROZEN:** their ultimate origin.

That gives future lore room to breathe.

---

## 6. Dragon taxonomy — FROZEN concept, not species list

"Dragon" is a broad human classification rather than one biological species.

Dragon Wake can contain different body plans.

| Class | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| **True Dragons** | Large, intelligent or near-intelligent ancient creatures. Frequently winged. Extremely rare. |
| **Wyrms** | Serpentine dragons. Generally wingless. Often associated with caves, mountains, underground places or ancient hoards. |
| **Wyverns** | Two-legged winged predators. More animalistic. Potentially more numerous than True Dragons. |
| **Drakes** | Ground-based dragon relatives. Smaller and more biologically animal-like. |
| **Sea Dragons** | Serpentine or leviathan-like creatures inhabiting oceans, rivers or deep lakes. |
| **Great Dragons** | Extremely old individuals whose age, intelligence and power place them beyond ordinary classifications. Individual characters in world history, not enemy tiers. |

---

## 7. Dragon elements — REVISED

Drop the conventional system: red = fire, blue = ice, green = poison,
yellow = lightning.

Different dragon species can possess unusual biological abilities, but those
abilities must feel grounded in their ecology.

Examples: combustible breath; venom; caustic secretion; extreme heat
tolerance; electrical discharge; poisonous blood; armored scales; underwater
respiration; exceptional hearing; camouflage.

The dragon is an animal or ancient being first. Its "element" is secondary.

---

## 8. Dragon anatomy — FROZEN

Dragons eventually use localized anatomy rather than only a health bar.

Important anatomical regions may include: head; eyes; throat; chest;
underbelly; forelimbs; hindlimbs; wings; wing membranes; tail; joints;
vital organs.

This creates actual anti-dragon tactics.

Example: damage wing → reduce flight → force landing → pikes become useful
→ Slayer gains access to vulnerable anatomy.

Different species can have different vulnerabilities.

---

## 9. Dragon intelligence — FROZEN

Dragons exist along an intelligence spectrum.

Some are essentially dangerous animals. Others demonstrate memory, planning,
recognition, communication, territorial agreements, vengeance, curiosity,
loyalty.

The oldest dragons may be clearly sapient.

Humans therefore argue over whether dragons are animals, monsters, gods,
people or something between them.

That debate should matter politically and religiously.

---

## 10. Dragon ownership — FROZEN

A True Dragon cannot simply be purchased or permanently "tamed."

The fundamental rule is:

> Humans may control beasts. Dragons choose alliances.

A human can capture a dragon. A human can imprison one. A human might
condition younger dragon species.

But a genuine bond with a True Dragon requires mutual acceptance.

This makes a Dragon Lord enormously prestigious.

---

## 11. Dragon Slayers — FROZEN

Dragon Slayers are not generic fantasy heroes.

They belong to traditions and institutions that developed because humans
needed ways to survive dragons.

Slayers rely on: accumulated bestiary knowledge; scouting; anatomy;
specialized equipment; preparation; teamwork; traps; terrain; specialized
formations.

Their greatest weapon is knowledge.

A legendary Slayer isn't someone with +9,000 attack. They're someone who
knows:

> An Ash Wyrm cannot turn its head properly immediately after releasing flame.

That knowledge can decide a battle.

---

## 12. Anti-dragon weapons — FROZEN philosophy

Historical weaponry forms the basis. Fantasy engineering evolves logically
from it.

**Ordinary weapons:** sword, spear, bow, longbow, crossbow, pike, halberd,
lance, axe, polearm.

**Anti-dragon developments:**

| Tool | Role |
| --- | --- |
| Wyrm Pike | Long reinforced infantry weapon for grounded dragons |
| Slayer Lance | Heavy lance optimized for penetrating joints or scale gaps |
| Great Arbalest | Extremely powerful mechanical crossbow |
| Dragon Bolt | Specialized heavy projectile |
| Hook Bolt | Designed to catch wings or membranes |
| Chain Harpoon | Used to restrict movement rather than simply deal damage |
| Dragon Net | Heavy reinforced net deployed by teams or engines |
| Slayer Shield | Specialized protection against heat, claws or corrosive attacks |
| Dragon Tower | Fortification designed to protect settlements from aerial attacks |

These should look like plausible medieval responses to dragons rather than
magical sci-fi weapons.

---

## 13. Human warfare remains important — FROZEN

Dragons must not invalidate normal armies.

Dragon Wake remains a kingdom warfare game.

The primary military roster should eventually include something similar to:

**Infantry:** Levy Spearman, Spearman, Pikeman, Shieldman, Man-at-Arms,
Halberdier.

**Ranged:** Shortbowman, Longbowman, Crossbowman, Heavy Crossbowman.

**Cavalry:** Scout Cavalry, Mounted Archer, Light Cavalry, Knight.

**Specialist:** Sapper, Engineer, Scout, Dragon Hunter, Dragon Slayer.

**Siege:** Battering Ram, Siege Tower, Trebuchet, Great Arbalest.

Different cultures may have distinct variations.

This list is a destination shape, not a requirement to 1:1 rename current
units. Design the medieval roster from first principles, then map it into
the resolver. See [`MIGRATION_PLAN.md`](./MIGRATION_PLAN.md).

---

## 14. Dragon hunting — FROZEN as its own gameplay mode

Dragon hunting should not simply be: march army → wait → battle report.

It eventually becomes an expedition system.

Discover → Scout → Identify species → Study Codex → Prepare equipment →
Select troops → Track dragon → Engage → React to behavior →
Kill / drive away / capture / communicate / bond →
Recover consequences and discoveries.

This gives Dragon Wake a gameplay loop that Dragons of Atlantis never fully
exploited.

---

## 15. Codex → Bestiary — FROZEN

Keep the Codex. Expand it into one of Dragon Wake's signature systems.

The player gradually records: dragon species; anatomy; habitats; behavior;
diet; migration; vulnerabilities; breath capabilities; known individuals;
historical encounters; recommended tactics; mythological accounts.

Knowledge can be incomplete or wrong.

Example, first encounter:

> Unknown Mountain Wyrm. Recorded encounters: 1. Wings: none observed.
> Armor: heavy. Known attacks: bite, tail. Vulnerabilities: unknown.

After several encounters:

> Ironback Wyrm. Ventral armor considerably weaker than dorsal scales.
> Avoid tightly packed infantry near its tail. Heavy crossbows recommended
> at <80 paces.

That can become actual mechanical progression.

Destination shape (not yet implemented): Bestiary, Arms & Warfare, and
Chronicle. See [`LORE_BIBLE_V1_BRIEF.md`](./LORE_BIBLE_V1_BRIEF.md).

---

## 16. Magic — FROZEN

Magic exists but is rare, mysterious, dangerous and poorly understood.

The average person cannot cast spells. Most armies have no battle mage.

Fantasy enters the world primarily through: dragons; ancient ruins; relics;
strange creatures; alchemy; curses; rituals; prophecy; rare magical
individuals; forgotten civilizations.

Magic should create wonder because it is uncommon.

Convenience items (speedups, shields) must not be framed as everyday time
magic. That is a lore/presentation problem, distinct from whether
deterministic F2P acceleration exists at all.

---

## 17. Human factions — MAJOR CHANGE FROZEN

The existing elemental identities:

- Brinecant
- Ashcoil
- Skyshear
- Mossvault

should no longer represent four simple elemental gameplay factions.

Those names can survive. But they should become things like: kingdoms;
ethnic cultures; ancient provinces; noble houses; geographic regions.

Civilizations should primarily be differentiated through:

history + culture + politics + military doctrine + relationship with dragons.

Placeholder examples (not canon):

- **The Slayer Kingdom** — dragons are existential threats. Strong heavy
  crossbows, fortified settlements and Slayer Orders.
- **The Sacred Kingdom** — dragons are ancient sacred beings. Killing one
  without cause is sacrilege.
- **The Dragon Lords** — extremely rare historic tradition of bonding with
  dragons. Other kingdoms fear them.
- **The Forge Realm** — harvests remains from dead dragons and develops
  specialized equipment.

Create original versions rather than these placeholders.

---

## 18. Politics around dragons — FROZEN

Dragon policy becomes one of the great ideological conflicts of the world.

Major positions include:

| Position | Claim |
| --- | --- |
| Extermination | Dragons cannot coexist with civilization. |
| Preservation | Dragons belong to the natural / world order. |
| Domination | Dragons should be captured and weaponized. |
| Bonding | Humans and dragons can coexist through mutual relationships. |
| Worship | Dragons are divine or semi-divine. |
| Study | Dragons should be understood rather than feared. |

This gives the world genuine political conflict.

---

## 19. Kingdom-building loop — FROZEN

Preserve the DoA-derived macro loop because it remains excellent:

Build settlement → produce resources → construct defenses → research →
recruit troops → explore world → fight PvE → discover dragons → conquer
territory → expand kingdom → join alliance → wage wars → encounter stronger
dragons → develop anti-dragon capabilities → eventually unlock dragon
relationships → establish additional settlements → become a regional power.

---

## 20. City fantasy — FROZEN

The player must eventually see an actual medieval settlement, not primarily
dashboard panels.

The city should visibly contain: castle / keep; walls; towers; barracks;
stables; workshops; smithies; markets; farms; homes; training fields; siege
yards; dragon defenses; eventually dragon-related structures.

Buildings visibly improve as they level.

This remains compatible with the existing underlying city simulation.

---

## 21. Persistent world — FROZEN

Dragon Wake remains a persistent multiplayer world.

Players occupy real positions. Distance matters. Resources matter. March
time matters. Territory matters. Neighbors matter. Alliances matter.

A dragon seen near one player's territory can potentially become a regional
event.

The world should eventually develop stories:

> "That Great Dragon destroyed three castles before House Valmere brought it down."

Those events can become part of server history.

---

## 22. PvP philosophy — FROZEN

PvP remains dangerous.

But the goal is strategic loss, not account destruction.

Wars should involve: troops; resources; castles; territory; wildernesses;
roads; strategic locations; alliance objectives; dragon-related sites.

Dragons may participate, but they should not reduce war to:

> whoever owns the stronger dragon wins.

Armies remain fundamental.

---

## 23. Alliances — FROZEN

The current Tideband system becomes a much larger concept.

Terminology can eventually become: House / Alliance / Order / Confederation
depending on lore.

Alliances eventually participate in: wars; diplomacy; territorial control;
shared objectives; dragon hunts; world bosses; trade; reinforcement;
intelligence sharing.

A Great Dragon appearing can become an alliance-scale military operation.

---

## 24. The player progression fantasy — FROZEN

The ideal progression curve becomes:

| Stage | Fantasy |
| --- | --- |
| Early game | I own a small castle. |
| Developing kingdom | I command hundreds of troops. |
| Regional lord | I command thousands and control territory. |
| Established power | I maintain several castles and influence neighboring rulers. |
| Dragon age | I can survive encounters with dragons. |
| Slayer age | I can deliberately hunt certain dragons. |
| Legendary ruler | A dragon knows my name. |
| Endgame | I command a kingdom whose actions affect dragons, nations and the history of the realm. |

That feels much more meaningful than simply increasing "Power" indefinitely.

---

## 25. Dragon progression — critically FROZEN

Acquiring a dragon should happen late enough that the player desperately
wants one.

Do not give every player a dragon five minutes into the tutorial.

The early game should make dragons mysterious.

The player might: hear rumors; see distant silhouettes; discover burned
villages; find footprints; recover scales; fight lesser drakes; study
records; watch powerful players with dragons.

Then eventually: encounter a True Dragon.

That makes the moment matter.

---

## 26. Dragon Wake name — KEEP

Keep Dragon Wake as the working product name.

The name directly signals the central premise: dragons are rare, dangerous,
and becoming active again. "Wake" is intentionally flexible while the Lore
Bible remains unfinished. It can mean an awakening, the scar or trail left
by a passing dragon, or the social alarm that spreads after a sighting.

Possible future interpretation (not yet canon):

> When the dragons woke, every kingdom learned to build for the sky.

The exact in-world meaning should be chosen in the Lore Bible. The name does
not require an ocean world or a return to the retired aquatic aesthetic.

---

## 27. Things explicitly removed from direction

Freeze these as **DO NOT BUILD** unless the direction is deliberately
reopened:

- elemental color factions
- thousands of dragons per army
- dragon gacha
- ubiquitous spellcasting
- generic fire / ice / earth / wind dragon taxonomy
- conventional RPG hero spam
- dragons treated as cosmetic mounts
- Power Score as the main strategy mechanic
- magical technology replacing medieval technology
- one-click dragon battles with no preparation
- factions distinguished primarily by percentage bonuses

This section is important because it prevents future AI agents from slowly
reintroducing generic genre conventions.

---

## 28. What remains from current Dragon Wake

Preserve the systems architecture conceptually.

Current: cities; plots / resources; queues; research; units; world map;
marches; deterministic combat; reports; wilderness; PvE camps; alliances;
chat; protection; persistence; events.

All remain useful.

The existing repo already proves this underlying MMORTS loop. The redesign
should therefore be evolutionary rather than a rewrite.

The biggest changes will eventually be: content model + world / lore +
progression + client architecture + visual presentation + dragon systems.

See [`MIGRATION_PLAN.md`](./MIGRATION_PLAN.md) for how "evolutionary" is
interpreted: domain-preserving migration, not a label swap. Schema, API
contracts, content IDs, progression and presentation will still move.

---

## 29. Canon layers

Treat future decisions according to four authority levels:

| Level | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **CANON — FROZEN** | Cannot casually change. Everything frozen above. |
| **CANON — PROVISIONAL** | Use until contradicted by deeper lore design. Names, kingdom structures, species names. |
| **LEGEND** | Stories believed by people in the world but not necessarily objectively true. Particularly useful for dragon origins. |
| **UNKNOWN** | Questions intentionally unanswered. This lets the setting retain mystery. |

Authority order across documents lives in
[`CANON_AUTHORITY.md`](./CANON_AUTHORITY.md).

---

## 30. The one-sentence direction

This is the sentence that keeps every future design decision aligned:

> Dragon Wake is a persistent medieval strategy world where historically grounded kingdoms have spent centuries adapting their armies, castles, cultures and beliefs to the reality of rare, terrifying and sometimes intelligent dragons.

And the product test becomes:

> Does this feature make Dragon Wake feel more like a believable medieval civilization shaped by dragons?

If yes, investigate it.

If it mainly makes Dragon Wake feel like a generic fantasy game, reject it.

---

## Freeze verdict

Everything through this point is Direction Freeze v1.0.

**Not frozen yet:** the exact world map, kingdom names, religions,
historical chronology, dragon species, creation myth, or first major war.

Those deserve Lore Bible v1, because prematurely locking them would give a
lot of canon but not necessarily good canon.

The next design campaign is:

Lore Bible v1 → world history → dragon taxonomy → kingdoms → religions →
Slayer Orders → warfare → first playable region → early-game narrative.

Only after that should the new direction be translated into a concrete
repository migration (Phase 2+ in [`MIGRATION_PLAN.md`](./MIGRATION_PLAN.md)).
